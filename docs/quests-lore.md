## The premise (the fiction every quest can lean on)

- The world is **Digimon Online**, a game run by the company **MAGAMI**. The player (called Junior in English) and friends log
  in; a system error traps everyone inside. So townspeople are *players* as well as programs: people with real lives outside.
- Friends: **Ivy** (renames herself **Kail**) and **Teddy**. Kail's long-lost older brother is the hacker **Lucky Mouse**
  (real name Kurt), secretly working against the villains.
- Villains: the **A.o.A.**, who control the West Sector; MAGAMI is a front for them. Their goal runs through the **Vemmon**
  Digi-Egg, the **Juggernaut**, **Destromon**, an **Oinkmon virus** that turns players into Oinkmon, and finally Lord
  Megadeath / Snatchmon / Galacticmon.
- Places named by the guides: **Asuka City** (the gate wants a pass; the **Staff Pass** and **ID passes** are plot), the
  **Asuka Sewers**, **Datamon's Control Room**, Lucky Mouse's **Secret Room**, the **Admin Center**, **Amaterasu Server and
  City** (post-game versions of places), the satellite **Gunslinger / MAGASTA**.
- Four city leaders by direction, **Seiryu** (east), **Suzaku** (south), **Byakko** (west), **Genbu** (north). Guide tidbits:
  Seiryu is reached by beating **MasterTyrannomon** on the tower for the **Old Claw**; Suzaku's team is Woodmon, RedVegiemon,
  ShogunGekomon; Byakko's first leader is a **fake, the real one is in the Mirage Tower**; Byakko's attacks stun, bring medicine.
- **Nick and Keith** (the sprites we call "Yellow Coat" and its partner, npc 102 and 103) are in the Suzaku underground-lake
  part of the plot. Treat them as a pair of rival-friends.
- After the ending, four new Server Leaders are set up and Kurt is World Champion (PAL/JP).

## Story hooks that fit side quests without touching the plot

A side quest must not upstage the main story and must never write story flags. These are things the fiction already makes
true, so they can be small human stories on its edge:

1. **People trapped in a game.** Someone is worried about a family member waiting outside; someone wants a message "to the
   real world" they know cannot go; someone has been logged in so long they forgot what they were here for.
2. **The Oinkmon virus.** Before it spreads, odd glitches: a player who keeps repeating one sentence, a duplicated vendor,
   an NPC walking in a loop. A mystery arc can be "what is wrong with Pelche's lamp-seller" and end at a harmless cause (a
   corrupted save) or a spooky one (someone's data is being copied).
3. **Rivals who share a screen.** Keith and Nick, Takato's goggles, the Yellow Coat's rematches: friendship under pressure.
4. **The admin side.** Staff passes, maintenance notices, an engineer who is behind on tickets. Office workers and
   researchers are the MAGAMI staff who are not villains.
5. **Lucky Mouse's network.** Hidden notes, secret doors, a hacker's drop box. Rewards are information and odd items.
6. **Leaders as people.** The four leaders are known only as fights. A quest can show one off duty, or show how a town feels
   about being ruled by a fake.
7. **Digimon as neighbours.** A Digimon wants to learn a technique, wants to be left alone, wants a lost friend found.

## Style rules that follow

- Quests should be small stories with a turn: the thing you were told is not quite the thing you find.
- Prefer *two or more kinds of step* (talk, then a fight, then a choice) over one repeated step.
- The reward is the story first; items second and fitting (a note, a card, a disk), never a flat pile of bits.
- Choices should matter a little (what the giver says next; who gets the item), and never gate story.
- Names: the disc names almost nobody. New characters get plain role-names or Digimon-style names, and a voice.
